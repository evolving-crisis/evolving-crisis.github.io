### 2.4. Comparison of paralog identification across different methods



We used the reference genome of *Sindora glabra* to identify the number and location of paralogs through a BLAST search using the assembled sequences (both HybPiper and CAPTUS) of taxa from the *Sindora* clade as queries. We limited our BLAST search to just the *Sindora* clade rather than the entire set of species from the Detarioideae clade because this method only detects paralogs present in the reference species *Sindora glabra*, and the results are less accurate the more divergent the taxa are from the reference species. While it is not possible to assess it here, this method could also leave out many potential paralogs from other species in the *Sindora* clade, although there are currently no other reference genomes available within the clade or tribe to allow a more accurate paralog identification across the taxa used here.

For both the HybPiper and CAPTUS-generated assemblies, the BLAST parameters used were `blastn` with an e-value of e-10, chosen to exclude less significant matches. The BLAST output was then filtered to retain only sequences of more than 90 bp in length, removing short matches which are likely to be false or partial. Genes with matches from multiple chromosomes were flagged as paralogous, while those with matches belonging to a single chromosome were manually checked for paralogs mapping to more than one region of the chromosome.

Pairwise comparisons of the paralogs identified with each pipeline (Fig. 1) were made with the respective (HybPiper/CAPTUS-based) BLAST search results. If any of the genes in the pairwise comparisons were not recovered or did not have any significant BLAST matches, the genes were excluded from the pairwise comparisons. These comparisons were represented as confusion matrices with relevant statistics of sensitivity and specificity using the R package `caret` v. 7.0-1.
