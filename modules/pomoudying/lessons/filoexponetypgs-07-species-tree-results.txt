### 3.5. Coalescent based and concatenated species trees



For the coalescent-based species trees generated by ASTRAL (Supplementary Figs. 5a–i), topologies were generally well-resolved across all datasets with most of the nodes being well-supported and with a Local Posterior Probability (LPP) score of 95% and above. Most of the nodes with moderate or low support were found within *Daniellia* and *Sindora*, although the node joining the tribes Schotieae and Barnebydendreae was poorly supported in all trees, and several nodes along the phylogenetic backbone were not well supported in the HybPiper PPD-modified and Y&S trees (Supplementary Figs. 5c, d, e, f). Species tree topologies were similar across all methods. Across all ASTRAL trees, topological differences were usually at the species level, for instance among the tips of the genera *Sindora*, *Daniellia*, *Copaifera* and *Guibourtia*. However, for the PPD-trimmed and HybPiper Y&S ASTRAL trees (Supplementary Figs. 5c, d, e, f), the Schotieae and Barnebydendreae tribes were resolved as sister to the rest of the Detarieae tribe as opposed to resolved as sister to the Saraceae, Afzelieae and Amherstieae tribes in all other ASTRAL trees (Supplementary Figs. 5a, b, g, h, i).

The concatenated trees (Supplementary Figs. 6a–i) generated using IQ-TREE produced well-supported phylogenetic trees for all datasets, with almost all nodes with 100% bootstrap support, while nodes with moderate or poor bootstrap support (<75%) were found mainly in *Sindora* and *Daniellia* and nodes subtending the Schotieae and Barnebydendreae tribes. Overall species tree topologies were similar at the backbone and genus level, with the exception of the Schotieae and Barnebydendreae tribes in relation to the other tribes. In the "HybPiper" and "HybPiper RemovedPPD77paralogs" datasets (Supplementary Figs. 6a, b), the Schotieae and Barnebydendreae were resolved as sisters, while in the HybPiper-based datasets with PPD- or Yang & Smith-modified alignments (Supplementary Figs. 6c, d, e, f), Schotieae was resolved as sister to Barnebydendreae which was in turn resolved as sister to the rest of the Detarieae. Finally, for all CAPTUS concatenated trees (Supplementary Figs. 6g, h, i), Barnebydendreae was resolved as sister to the rest of the Detarioideae, followed by Schotieae and then the three tribes Saraceae, Afzelieae and Amherstieae. However, at the species level within *Sindora*, the phylogenetic relationships among the species were more variable in the concatenated trees, with groupings not shared across all nine trees except for the relationships (*Sindora laotica*, *S. glabra*) and (*S. coriacea*, (*S. velutina*, *S. beccariana*)) (Table 5).

### 3.6. Quartet sampling shows that support for alternative topologies is clustered in certain genera



Across all datasets, most nodes had positive QC values (nodes in green and white) which showed that there were more quartets in agreement than disagreement with the topology (Fig. 6, Supplementary Fig. 7a–i). However, along the phylogenetic backbone for all quartet sampling trees (Supplementary Fig. 7a–i), there were more white nodes ($0 < QC \le 0.5$) than green nodes ($QC > 0.5$), showing that the proportion of concordant quartets did not exceed the discordant quartets by a large majority. Nodes with negative QC values (yellow and red nodes), showing more discordant than concordant quartets, were generally concentrated within the genera *Sindora* and *Daniellia* (Supplementary Fig. 7a–i), within the Amherstieae tribe (Supplementary Fig. 7d) and also along the backbone at nodes leading to the Schotieae and Barnebydendreae (Supplementary Fig. 7d, e).

Quartet Differential (QD) values measure the distribution of the discordant quartets, so the focus was on the yellow and red nodes ($QC < 0$) which had more discordant than concordant quartets. Across all datasets, the QD values of these higher-discordance nodes were closer to 1 than to 0, showing that the discordant quartets tended to be more equally distributed (Supplementary Figs. 7a–i).

Quartet Informativeness (QI) values were generally variable in all datasets, ranging from 1 to close to 0, and nodes with a low proportion of informative replicates were located both along the phylogenetic backbone and also close to the tips of the tree. However, among all the trees, the two PPD-trimmed trees (Supplementary Figs. 7c and d) showed lower QI values when compared to the equivalent branch in the QS results of the other datasets.
