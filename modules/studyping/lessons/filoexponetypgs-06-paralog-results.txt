## 3. Results



### 3.1. Summary of sequence recovery from HybPiper and CAPTUS



HybPiper recovered a total of 24,229 genes across all loci and all samples, 2,314 more genes than the 21,915 recovered by CAPTUS for their respective default datasets (Table 2, Supplementary Fig. 1–2). However, CAPTUS tended to recover more variable and parsimony informative sites in the alignments despite the lower number of genes recovered (Table 2).

**Table 2.** Gene Recovery and Alignment Statistics for HybPiper and CAPTUS.

| Method | Total Genes Recovered | Average Genes Recovered | Total Alignment Length | No. of Variable Sites | No. of Parsimony Informative Sites | Percentage Gaps/Missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HybPiper | 24,229 | 275/289 | 544,374 | 387,539 | 265,945 | 29.5%

 |
| CAPTUS | 21,915 | 249/289 | 776,594 | 474,380 | 289,938 | 52.2%

 |

### 3.2. Comparison of paralog recovery methods using the *Sindora* clade samples



#### 3.2.1. Comparison of number of paralogs recovered from the HybPiper and CAPTUS pipelines



A total of 71 genes were identified as paralogous by HybPiper (Fig. 3). For CAPTUS, 286 genes were found to have paralogs in at least one sample, which are more evenly distributed across the samples and the genes (Fig. 4).

*Fig. 3. Heatmap of paralogs recovered from HybPiper. Each sample occupies one row, and genes are represented by columns. For each sample, genes with paralogs are indicated by dark blue boxes, with darker colours indicating a larger number of paralogs, and the maximum number being 14. The full figure with sample and gene names is available in Supplementary Fig. 3.*

*Fig. 4. Heatmap of paralogs recovered from CAPTUS. Each sample occupies one row, and genes are represented by columns. For each sample, genes with paralogs are indicated by dark blue boxes, with darker colours indicating a larger number of paralogs, and the maximum number being 14. The full figure with sample and gene names is available in Supplementary Fig. 4.*

#### 3.2.2. Paralog identification using the Putative paralog detection (PPD) pipeline



For the set of 25 samples from the *Sindora* clade, with the default value of 50% shared heterozygous sites across gene alignments, the PPD pipeline did not find any paralogous genes at `bwa mem` $k=100$ and only 5 at $k=60$, but found 41 paralogous genes at $k=19$ (Table 3). For the 88 Detarioideae clade samples, when the percentage of shared heterozygous sites was reduced to 14%, `bwa mem` $k=19$ similarly yielded the largest number of paralogous genes (Table 3).

**Table 3.** Number of genes identified as paralogous using the PPD pipeline from the 25 *Sindora* clade samples and 88 Detarioideae samples.

| `bwa-mem` $k$ value | 25 samples (*Sindora* clade): Sites shared across 50% (13/25 individuals) | 88 samples (Detarioideae): Sites shared across 14% (13/88 individuals) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 0 | 1

 |
| 60 | 5 | 8

 |
| 19 | 41 | 77

### 3.4. Assessing accuracy of paralog identification across different methods



PPD showed the closest match in identifying paralogs compared to the BLAST search against the reference genome, followed closely by CAPTUS, despite the large difference in number of paralogous genes identified (41 vs 195) (Table 4). CAPTUS also showed the highest sensitivity in identifying paralogs, followed by PPD and then HybPiper. In terms of specificity, both HybPiper and PPD showed higher specificity than CAPTUS.
